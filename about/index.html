Sailing

Sailing has been a big passion of mine for years. It was always a fun and competitive way to get outside and get exercise. I started racing going into high school, and by my senior year my team qualified for 2021 nationals in Cleveland, OH, and later the Sears cup in Marblehead, MA. I did not want to stop there, so I joined the University of Hawaii sailing team and began a four year period that involved countless regattas across California and as far as Boston, MA, where I competed in the college nationals in 2024. The next spring, I drove our 8 manned big-boat team to a 2nd place finish at the Port of LA Harbor cup. I led the team to numerous podium finishes at divisional regattas, winning my fleet at the district championship in 2025, which had not been done by a UH sailor since 2007. That fall, I was selected for the college singlehanded nationals in Norfolk, VA.

These experiences brought me several valuable skills, such as leadership, resilience, performance under pressure, and a persitant work ethic. Navigating team dynamics and fostering a positive environment is crucial for success. Sailing is unlike any other sport where the course and conditions vary wildly between venues and even from day to day. That adversity instills an adaptability that reaches into many other disciplines. When a small mistake can be costly, focus and regulation of nerves on the water is a must. The skill curve of sailing is enormously vast because it covers such a wide range of skills all bundled into one. Maneuvers, speed, strategy, tactics, wind sense, and the ability to make decisions at a moment's notice are what make sailing one of the most multi faceted sports in the world. This requires continuous structured practice, and self identification of areas to improve.

Sailing has been really good for my personal development and continues to teach me how to apply myself in new ways.
